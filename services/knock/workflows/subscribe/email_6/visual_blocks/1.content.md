If you've been moved into a walking boot, this is where the real healing happens. The boot protects your tendon while allowing controlled loading. Understanding how it works helps you follow your protocol correctly.

## Boot Basics

**Your walking boot:**

- Keeps your ankle in the correct position (pointed down) while your tendon heals
- Many protocols have you wear it **24/7 for several weeks** (timing varies)
- Follow your protocol exactly — don't rush ahead

**The angle system:**

- You start at maximum angle (all wedges in for Aircast, or hinge locked for VACOped)
- Your clinician will tell you when to reduce the angle (**wedge removal timeline**)
- Never adjust without instruction

## Your Early Routine

Keep it simple:

- **Elevate** your ankle above heart level as much as possible
- **Protect the tendon 24/7** — no stretching, no barefoot testing
- **Take any prescribed blood thinners** as directed
- **Move carefully** on crutches and slow down on stairs and in the bathroom
- **Know the red flags** — blood clots (DVT or PE) or circulation problems (numb/blue toes) need urgent help

## Sleep and washing

Many people struggle to sleep in a bulky walking boot. If your clinician allows a lighter night option, some patients use a lightweight night splint so the ankle stays pointed down without the bulk — ask your team what is safe for your protocol. Our [night splint page](https://thetismedical.com/splint) explains how that option works if relevant to you.
